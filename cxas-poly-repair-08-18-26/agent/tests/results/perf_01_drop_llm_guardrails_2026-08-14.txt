WHY THE TURN TOTAL PROVES NOTHING HERE. Pooled sd is 573ms, so at n=20 per arm this design
has 80% power to detect ~359ms. The saving is 255ms. It is below the floor by construction,
and the two batches disagreed on sign (-514ms then +79ms) — which is exactly what noise at
this scale looks like, and is the reason a single batch would have been reported as a win.

WHAT IS NEVERTHELESS PROVEN. The guardrail component is a direct measurement, not an
inference from a total: 255ms of serialized LLM judging on every prose turn, gone in 20 of
20 runs with no overlap between arms. Per rule, from the earlier single-drive breakdown:
Competitor Mention 336ms, Prompt Guardrail 335ms, Unprompted Credit 238ms; the three
deterministic matchers were 0-1ms and remain.

THE REAL FINDING. Framework variance (sd 573ms) exceeds the entire guardrail budget. No
guardrail change could have been proven end-to-end against that background, and nothing
else will be either until the framework's per-turn cost comes down. That is the argument
for doing the SDK work next rather than more guardrail work.

The confounder worth keeping: engine re-entries per drive are chosen by the MODEL (one per
dispatched task) and each is worth ~250ms, so an arm that happens to dispatch one more task
looks 250ms slower for reasons unrelated to the change. Medians matched at 13 here. Any
future comparison must report this alongside the timings.
